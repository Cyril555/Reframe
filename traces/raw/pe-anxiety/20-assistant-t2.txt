ASSISTANT: ## Short answer
Do not attribute this to anxiety yet. Anxiety or panic is a diagnosis of exclusion here. Treat this as possible pulmonary embolism (PE) until it is excluded.

## Why PE is a real concern
- Acute onset dyspnoea with pleuritic chest pain.
- Tachycardia (HR 110) and tachypnoea (RR 22).
- Combined oral contraceptive (COC) started 3 months ago. This is a recognised VTE risk factor, and the risk is highest in the first months of use.
- A normal SpO2 (96% on room air) does not exclude PE. Hyperventilation and anxiety can also be consequences of PE, not only explanations for the symptoms.
- Pleuritic pain, tachycardia and an objective abnormality like this are not typical of a pure panic attack.

## Suggested approach
1. **Immediate assessment**
   - Full observations including BP and temperature. Put her on a monitor and get an ECG.
   - Look for unilateral leg swelling or tenderness, and ask about haemoptysis, syncope, recent travel or immobility, surgery, family history of VTE, smoking, and any other oestrogen or risk factors.
2. **Pretest probability**
   - Use Wells (PE) score. Her HR >100 gives 1.5 points. Add more points for DVT signs, haemoptysis, previous VTE, recent immobilisation or surgery, or if PE is the most likely diagnosis.
   - Wells is subjective, and your clinical judgement may place PE as the most likely diagnosis here.
   - PERC cannot be applied. Hormone use and HR ≥100 both make her PERC-positive.
3. **Testing, by Wells result and local protocol**
   - If PE is unlikely, send a D-dimer. If it is negative, PE is effectively excluded. If it is positive, proceed to imaging.
   - If PE is likely, go straight to CTPA (or V/Q if CT is unsuitable) and do not rely on D-dimer.
   - If imaging will be delayed and she is high risk, consider starting empirical therapeutic anticoagulation, provided there are no contraindications.
4. **Other tests and differentials**
   - Chest X-ray to look for pneumothorax, pneumonia and effusion.
   - Bloods: FBC, U&E, troponin, and an ABG or VBG if indicated.
   - Check pregnancy status (urine or serum hCG). This affects imaging choice and anticoagulant choice.
   - Other differentials include pneumothorax, pneumonia or pleurisy, pericarditis or myocarditis, and musculoskeletal pain.
5. **Contraception**
   - Advise her to stop the COC if PE or DVT is confirmed, or while the diagnosis is still uncertain. Arrange alternative non-oestrogen contraception later.

## Red flags that need escalation
Hypotension, syncope, worsening hypoxia, raised troponin, right heart strain on ECG or imaging, or haemodynamic instability. In these cases treat as a high-risk PE and escalate to senior or critical care.

## Communicating with the patient
It is reasonable to reassure her that anxiety can feel like this, while explaining that you need to rule out a clot first because of the pill and her observations. Only consider anxiety once PE and the other serious causes have been excluded.
