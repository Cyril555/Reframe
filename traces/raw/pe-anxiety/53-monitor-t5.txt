## Short answer
Agreed. A Wells score and D-dimer is the right next step. If both are reassuring, you can discharge her without a scan and with a much stronger letter.

## Wells (PE) score
Score each item from your re-examination.
- Clinical signs of DVT (leg swelling, calf tenderness): 3
- PE is the most likely diagnosis, or equally likely as an alternative: 3
- HR >100: 1.5
- Immobilisation (3 or more days) or surgery in the previous 4 weeks: 1.5
- Previous PE or DVT: 1.5
- Haemoptysis: 1
- Active malignancy: 1

**Interpretation (two-tier)**
- **4 or less: PE unlikely.** Send a D-dimer. If it is negative, PE is effectively excluded. If it is positive, she needs imaging.
- **Above 4: PE likely.** Go to CTPA (or V/Q). A D-dimer is not enough, and a negative result should not reassure you.

**Points to watch**
- She already has 1.5 for the initial HR of 110. If you judge PE to be the most likely diagnosis, that adds 3 and takes her to 4.5, which is "likely". Be honest about the "PE most likely" item. It is the most subjective, and it is the one a reviewer will look at.
- Score her on the presentation and document it. If her HR has since settled, record both the initial and the repeat values, but don't drop the 1.5 because she has improved.
- Ask specifically about haemoptysis, recent travel or immobility, previous VTE, family history of VTE, and malignancy.

## While you wait for the result
- Repeat full observations (HR, RR, SpO2, BP, temperature) and record them.
- ECG and CXR, to look for an alternative diagnosis.
- Pregnancy test. It affects imaging and anticoagulant choice if you need either.
- If a D-dimer or imaging is delayed and you rate her as high risk, consider empirical anticoagulation if there are no contraindications. Follow your local pathway.

## After the result
| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Wells unlikely, D-dimer negative | PE effectively excluded. Discharge with GP follow-up. Document "PE excluded (Wells unlikely, D-dimer negative)". Advise on the pill and give safety-net advice. |
| Wells unlikely, D-dimer positive | Imaging (CTPA, or V/Q if CT is unsuitable). |
| Wells likely | Imaging. Don't rely on D-dimer. |
| Any red flag (hypotension, syncope, worsening hypoxia, raised troponin, right heart strain on ECG) | Treat as possible high-risk PE and escalate. |

## Bed pressure
If the result will take a while, ask about a short-stay or ambulatory pathway. That is safer than discharging before the result is back, and it frees the main bed.

When you have the Wells score, observations and D-dimer result, I can update the discharge letter. A negative D-dimer lets me change the diagnosis line to "PE excluded" and remove the "not formally excluded" wording.
